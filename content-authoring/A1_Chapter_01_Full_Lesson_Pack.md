# LinguaTrack Academy — A1 Chapter 01: Greetings, Introductions & Jobs

**Status:** Editorial authoring source; **not** a validated curriculum-v2 package or approved release.  
**Learner:** CEFR A1 beginner; English teaching text with Burmese support.  
**Estimated study time:** 2.5–4 hours across several days.  
**Target skills:** Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing; supporting Grammar and Vocabulary.  
**Purpose:** Say hello, give a basic introduction, ask about names/jobs, and understand short introductions.

## Chapter learning outcomes

After completing the chapter, the learner should be able to **practice** the following:
1. Greet a person politely and say goodbye.
2. Introduce themselves using name, country and job in 3–5 simple sentences.
3. Use `I/you/he/she/it/we/they` with `am/is/are` in simple affirmative statements.
4. Ask and answer `What is your name?`, `Where are you from?`, and `What do you do?`.
5. Understand a short spoken introduction with familiar vocabulary and clear audio.
6. Read a short self-introduction and find explicit facts.
7. Write 5–7 coherent A1-level sentences about themselves.

# Lesson 1 — Hello! Greetings and Useful Words

**Duration:** 20–25 min | **Area:** Vocabulary + spoken interaction

### Explanation

In English, greetings depend on the situation. `Hi` and `Hello` are both common. `Good morning` is a polite greeting in the morning. `Nice to meet you` is useful when you meet a person for the first time.

**မြန်မာလို:** လူသစ်နဲ့တွေ့တဲ့အခါ `Hello` / `Hi` လို့နှုတ်ဆက်နိုင်တယ်။ မနက်ပိုင်းမှာ `Good morning` လို့ ပြောလို့ရတယ်။ ပထမဆုံးတွေ့ရင် `Nice to meet you` (တွေ့ရတာဝမ်းသာပါတယ်) လို့ ပြောတတ်ဖို့ အရေးကြီးတယ်။

### Vocabulary

| English | Burmese meaning | A1 example |
|---|---|---|
| Hello / Hi | မင်္ဂလာပါ / ဟိုင်း | Hello, I'm Min. |
| Good morning | မင်္ဂလာနံနက်ခင်းပါ | Good morning, Aye. |
| Nice to meet you | တွေ့ရတာ ဝမ်းသာပါတယ် | Nice to meet you, too. |
| My name is... | ကျွန်တော့်/ကျွန်မ နာမည်က… | My name is Su. |
| I'm... | ကျွန်တော်/ကျွန်မက… | I'm a developer. |
| from | …က လာတာ / …က ဖြစ်တာ | I'm from Myanmar. |
| work | အလုပ်လုပ်သည် | I work in Tokyo. |
| a developer | ဆော့ဖ်ဝဲ/ဝဘ်ရေးသူ | I am a developer. |
| a designer | ဒီဇိုင်နာ | She is a designer. |
| a manager | မန်နေဂျာ | He is a manager. |
| colleague | လုပ်ဖော်ကိုင်ဖက် | She is my colleague. |
| Goodbye / See you | နုတ်ဆက်ပါတယ် / နောက်မှတွေ့မယ် | See you tomorrow. |

**Teacher note:** `I'm` means `I am`. `I'm a developer` requires `a` before the singular job noun.

### Mini-dialogue

**A:** Hello! I'm Min. What's your name?  
**B:** Hi, Min. My name is Su.  
**A:** Nice to meet you, Su.  
**B:** Nice to meet you, too.

**မြန်မာလို အဓိပ္ပာယ်:** ကိုယ့်နာမည် ပြောပြီး တစ်ဖက်လူရဲ့နာမည်ကို မေးတယ်။ ပထမဆုံးတွေ့လို့ ဝမ်းသာကြောင်း ပြောတယ်။

### Practice — choose one

**V1.** You meet someone for the first time. Which phrase is best?  
A. Nice to meet you. | B. Good night, yesterday. | C. I name is Min.

**V2.** Complete: `My name ___ Su.`  
A. am | B. is | C. are

**V3.** Which sentence describes a job correctly?  
A. I'm developer. | B. I a developer. | C. I'm a developer.

**V4.** A colleague says, “Nice to meet you.” Choose a suitable reply.  
A. Nice to meet you, too. | B. I name is job. | C. You from?

**V5.** Which word means a person you work with?  
A. colleague | B. country | C. morning

### Production task (not graded)

Say aloud: `Hello. My name is ____. I'm from ____. I'm a ____. Nice to meet you.`

# Lesson 2 — Grammar: Who Are You?

**Duration:** 30–40 min | **Area:** Grammar + sentence construction

**Integration:** Existing `be` and subject-pronoun lessons cover much of this material. Link or enrich those lessons instead of blindly duplicating published or draft IDs.

### Rule A: Subject pronouns

| Subject | `be` | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | am | I am a developer. |
| You | are | You are my colleague. |
| He | is | He is a manager. |
| She | is | She is a designer. |
| It | is | It is a laptop. |
| We | are | We are developers. |
| They | are | They are students. |

**မြန်မာလို:** ဝါကျရဲ့ ကတ္တား (Subject) နဲ့ ကိုက်တဲ့ `am/is/are` ကို သုံးရတယ်။ `I am`, `you/we/they are`, `he/she/it is` ကို အရင်မှတ်ပါ။ `You` က လူတစ်ယောက်ကို ပြောနေရင်လည်း `are` ပါပဲ။

### Rule B: Sentence structure

- **Subject + be + complement:** `She` (subject) + `is` (be) + `a designer` (who she is).
- **Subject + action verb + object:** `I` (subject) + `write` (verb) + `code` (object).

**မြန်မာလို:** `She is a designer` မှာ `a designer` က သူဘယ်သူဖြစ်လဲဆိုတာ ဖြည့်ဖော်ပြတဲ့ Complement ဖြစ်တယ်။ `I write code` မှာ `code` က ရေးတဲ့အရာ (Object) ဖြစ်တယ်။ Sentence တိုင်းမှာ Object မလိုပါ — `I work.` လည်း ပြည့်စုံတယ်။

### Rule C: `a` / `an` with one job

- `I am **a** developer.`
- `She is **an** engineer.`
- `He is **a** manager.`

**မြန်မာလို:** ရေတွက်လို့ရတဲ့ အလုပ်အကိုင်နာမ် တစ်ခုတည်းရှေ့မှာ အများအားဖြင့် `a` ဒါမှမဟုတ် `an` သုံးတယ်။ `an` ကို အစမှာ သရအသံထွက်တဲ့ စကားလုံးတွေအရှေ့မှာ သုံးတယ် (`an engineer`)။

### Rule D: Basic questions and answers

- `What is your name?` — `My name is Min.`
- `Where are you from?` — `I'm from Myanmar.`
- `What do you do?` — `I'm a web developer.`
- `Are you a designer?` — `No, I'm not. I'm a developer.`

**မြန်မာလို:** `What do you do?` ဆိုတာ ဒီအခြေအနေမှာ **ဘာအလုပ်လုပ်လဲ** လို့မေးတာပါ။ `What are you doing?` ဆိုတဲ့ အခုဘာလုပ်နေလဲ ဆိုတာနဲ့ မတူဘူး။

### Common mistakes

| Incorrect | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I is a developer. | I am a developer. | `I` + `am` |
| She are a manager. | She is a manager. | `she` + `is` |
| I am developer. | I am a developer. | `a` before one job noun |
| I from Myanmar. | I am from Myanmar. | Use `am` |
| He write code. | He writes code. | Present simple `he` + `-s` |
| I am write code. | I write code. | No `am` before base present-simple action verb |

### Practice

**G1.** `He ___ a designer.` A. am | B. are | C. is

**G2.** `They ___ colleagues.` A. are | B. is | C. am

**G3.** Choose correct: A. I'm engineer. | B. I'm an engineer. | C. I an engineer.

**G4.** `___ you from Japan?` A. Is | B. Am | C. Are

**G5.** Choose correct: A. I write code. | B. I am write code. | C. I code write.

**G6.** `She ___ code.` A. write | B. writes | C. writing

**G7.** In `She is a designer`, `a designer` is: A. subject | B. complement | C. action object

**G8.** In `I read books`, `books` is: A. object | B. subject | C. verb

**G9.** `My name ___ Aye.` A. are | B. am | C. is

**G10.** Choose correct: A. He a manager is. | B. He is a manager. | C. Is he a manager.

### Free production (ungraded)

Write three sentences about yourself using: (1) `I am...`, (2) `I am from...`, (3) `I + action verb + object`. Example: `I am a developer. I am from Myanmar. I write code.`

# Lesson 3 — Listening: Meet the New Colleague

**Duration:** 25–30 min | **Area:** Listening comprehension

**IMPORTANT:** Use actual audio. Learners should listen without seeing the transcript first. The scripts below are for generating/recording audio and teacher review. Recommended speech: natural clear A1 speed with brief pauses; do not mark this as assessed listening until audio playback and transcript concealment work.

## Audio L1 — First day at work (about 30 seconds)

**Speaker A (Min):** Good morning. I'm Min. What's your name?  
**Speaker B (Anna):** Hi, Min. My name is Anna. Nice to meet you.  
**Min:** Nice to meet you, too. Are you a designer?  
**Anna:** No, I'm not. I'm a developer.  
**Min:** Great! I'm a developer, too.

### Questions (listen twice before checking)

**L1.** What is the new colleague's name? A. Anna | B. Aye | C. Mina

**L2.** What does Anna do? A. Designer | B. Developer | C. Manager

**L3.** Is Min also a developer? A. Yes | B. No | C. Not mentioned

**L4.** Which greeting do they use? A. Good morning | B. Good evening | C. Goodbye

## Audio L2 — Short personal introduction (about 25 seconds)

**Speaker (Su):** Hello. My name is Su. I'm from Myanmar. I live in Japan. I work in Tokyo. I'm a web designer. My colleague Ken is a developer. We work together. Nice to meet you.

### Questions

**L5.** Where is Su from? A. Japan | B. Myanmar | C. Korea

**L6.** Where does Su work? A. Osaka | B. Yangon | C. Tokyo

**L7.** What is Su's job? A. Web designer | B. Project manager | C. Engineer

**L8.** What is Ken's job? A. Teacher | B. Developer | C. Student

### Transcript study (after first attempts)

Read the scripts above while replaying the audio, underline `I'm`, `is`, `are`, and repeat three phrases aloud.

**မြန်မာလို:** အသံကို စာသားမကြည့်ဘဲ အရင်နှစ်ခေါက်နားထောင်ပါ။ မေးခွန်းတွေဖြေပြီးမှ Transcript ကိုဖွင့်ပြီး တစ်ကြောင်းချင်း ပြန်နားထောင်ပါ။

# Lesson 4 — Speaking: Introduce Yourself at Work

**Duration:** 25–35 min | **Area:** Speaking practice (self-reviewed, not graded)

### Speaking patterns

- `Hello, my name is ____.`
- `I'm from ____.`
- `I live in ____.`
- `I'm a ____.`
- `I work in ____.`
- `Nice to meet you.`

### Task S1 — 30-second introduction

Use 4–6 short sentences. Example:

> Hello! My name is Min. I'm from Myanmar. I live in Japan. I'm a web developer. I work in Tokyo. Nice to meet you.

### Task S2 — Role-play (two turns each)

**A:** Hello! What's your name?  
**B:** My name is ____. What's your name?  
**A:** I'm ____. Where are you from?  
**B:** I'm from ____. Nice to meet you.

Then switch roles and ask `What do you do?`

### Task S3 — Repair a mistake aloud

Say the corrected sentence naturally three times:
- `I is a developer.` → `I am a developer.`
- `She are a manager.` → `She is a manager.`
- `I from Myanmar.` → `I am from Myanmar.`

### Self-review checklist

- [ ] I said my name clearly.
- [ ] I said where I am from.
- [ ] I said my job using `a/an` correctly.
- [ ] I used `am/is/are` correctly in the prepared sentences.
- [ ] I could say the introduction without reading every word by the final try.

**မြန်မာလို:** Grammar မှားမှာ ကြောက်လို့ မရပ်လိုက်နဲ့။ ပထမအကြိမ် အဆုံးထိ ပြောပါ။ ဒုတိယအကြိမ်မှာ အမှား ၁–၂ ခု ပြင်ပါ။ ဒီ Checklist က Self-practice အတွက်သာ ဖြစ်ပြီး Speaking CEFR Score မဟုတ်ပါ။

# Lesson 5 — Reading: Our New Team Members

**Duration:** 20–25 min | **Area:** Reading comprehension

### Passage A (A1)

Hello! My name is Maya. I'm from India. I live in Tokyo. I'm a designer. I work with two developers, Min and Ken. Min is from Myanmar. Ken is from Japan. We are colleagues. We work on websites.

**မြန်မာလို (ဖတ်ပြီးမှကြည့်ပါ):** Maya က India ကဖြစ်ပြီး Tokyo မှာနေတယ်။ Designer ဖြစ်တယ်။ Min နဲ့ Ken ဆိုတဲ့ Developer နှစ်ယောက်နဲ့ အလုပ်လုပ်တယ်။ သူတို့က Website တွေကို အတူတူလုပ်ကြတယ်။

### Questions

**R1.** Where is Maya from? A. India | B. Japan | C. Myanmar

**R2.** What is Maya's job? A. Manager | B. Designer | C. Teacher

**R3.** Who is from Myanmar? A. Ken | B. Maya | C. Min

**R4.** How many developers are named? A. One | B. Three | C. Two

**R5.** What do they work on? A. Websites | B. Cars | C. Books

### Passage B (A1)

My name is Ken. I am a developer. I work in Tokyo. Anna is my colleague. She is a developer, too. Our manager is Mr. Sato. He is kind. We are a small team.

**R6.** Is Anna a developer? A. Yes | B. No | C. Not stated

**R7.** Who is the manager? A. Anna | B. Mr. Sato | C. Ken

**R8.** Is the team small? A. No | B. Not stated | C. Yes

### Reading strategy

Underline names, places, and jobs. Answer questions using **only** information clearly stated in the passage, not guesses.

**မြန်မာလို:** ဖတ်တဲ့အခါ နာမည်၊ နေရာ၊ အလုပ်အကိုင် စကားလုံးတွေကို အရင်ရှာပါ။ စာထဲမှာမပါတဲ့ အချက်ကို ကိုယ့်ဘာသာ မခန့်မှန်းပါနဲ့။

# Lesson 6 — Writing: My First Professional Introduction

**Duration:** 25–35 min | **Area:** Writing production (self-reviewed, not graded)

### Build a paragraph

Write **5–7 short sentences**, using the prompts:
1. What is your name?
2. Where are you from?
3. Where do you live?
4. What do you do?
5. Where do you work? (optional)
6. What do you like? (optional)
7. A polite closing.

### Model answer (about 40 words)

Hello! My name is Min. I'm from Myanmar. I live in Japan. I'm a front-end web developer. I work on websites. I like learning new things. Nice to meet you.

### Burmese explanation

`I'm a front-end web developer` မှာ `a` လိုတယ်။ `I work on websites` လို့ပြောတာက Website တွေပေါ်မှာ အလုပ်လုပ်တယ်ဆိုတဲ့ သဘာဝကျတဲ့ အသုံးအနှုန်းပါ။ ဝါကျအစမှာ စာလုံးကြီးသုံးပြီး အဆုံးမှာ `.` ထည့်ပါ။

### Guided writing prompts

**W1.** Finish: `My name ____ ____.`  
**W2.** Finish: `I ____ from ____.`  
**W3.** Finish: `I ____ a ____.`

### Self-edit checklist

- [ ] Each sentence begins with a capital letter.
- [ ] Each statement ends with a full stop.
- [ ] I use `I am / I'm`, not `I is`.
- [ ] I use `a/an` before a singular job noun.
- [ ] My sentences are understandable and the information is about me.

Write a second version without looking at the model. Compare both drafts and note one improvement. **The website should eventually save both versions**, but this source pack alone cannot persist them.

# Lesson 7 — Chapter Review & Assessment Blueprint

**Duration:** 25–40 min | **Assessment scope:** Knowledge checks + self-practice, **not** CEFR certification.

### Knowledge check (10 MCQs)

**T1.** `I ___ a developer.` A. am | B. is | C. are

**T2.** `She ___ from Japan.` A. are | B. is | C. am

**T3.** `They ___ my colleagues.` A. is | B. am | C. are

**T4.** Choose correct: A. I'm manager. | B. I'm a manager. | C. I manager.

**T5.** Which asks about a person's job? A. Where are you from? | B. What's your name? | C. What do you do?

**T6.** `___ you a web developer?` A. Are | B. Is | C. Am

**T7.** Choose correct: A. I am like tea. | B. I like tea. | C. I tea like.

**T8.** `My colleague ___ a designer.` A. am | B. are | C. is

**T9.** Choose correct: A. We work on websites. | B. We works on websites. | C. We working websites.

**T10.** Choose a polite first-meeting phrase. A. Who are now? | B. Nice to meet you. | C. You job is?

### Skill evidence tasks

- **Listening:** Replay L1 or use a fresh parallel dialogue with audio, then answer four unseen comprehension questions. Don't reuse transcript-visible practice as an unseen assessment.
- **Speaking:** Record a 30–45-second introduction and answer two live or recorded questions. Self-check clarity and basic structures; store as `self_practice` unless reviewed.
- **Reading:** Read a **new** 60–90-word introduction and answer five explicit-fact questions. (Use an authored unseen parallel text before formal assessment.)
- **Writing:** Write 5–7 original sentences without copying a model. Save and review manually or with a future authorized reviewer workflow.

### Chapter completion vs proficiency

**Suggested learner progress:** mark all learning activities complete after actual participation. **Suggested quiz progress:** record knowledge-check accuracy for T1–T10. Do **not** automatically infer CEFR level or writing/speaking proficiency. Thresholds for a production chapter assessment require a separate reviewed assessment policy and a server-side grading implementation.
